# Independent numerical QA of the previous implementation

Reviewed 15 September 2026. This review concerns the existing `shower-curtain-lab` solver and supplies design checks for phase 1. It does not approve a replacement solver that has not yet been examined. The original article and repository were left unchanged.

## Findings

The previous calculation is an authentic implementation of its stated regularized equations. I found no reversed sign in the reported inward traction. Its grid sensitivity and outward spray result cannot be repaired responsibly by reversing a sign or choosing a stronger arbitrary downward source.

The implemented strip mechanics are suitable to retain. I independently reran all 12 existing tests; they passed. In particular, the strip stiffness, manufactured static solution, gravity/hem terms, projection identities, and discrete force/work identities pass their existing checks. These are useful numerical checks, but do not establish accurate pressure loads for the room simulation.

| Issue | Direct evidence or derivation | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Traction sign is consistent | Sheet position is `x = curtain_x - y`; traction `F` acts on air. Reaction on the sheet is `-F`, whose component along inward `-e_x` is `F_x`. | Positive `traction[0]` correctly means inward sheet load. |
| Strong grid-dependent numerical diffusion | Momentum and scalar transport use first-order upwind fluxes. In a locally constant one-dimensional transport speed, leading added diffusivity is approximately `abs(U) h / 2` (with a CFL-dependent correction for explicit time integration). At `U = 1 m/s` and `h = 0.03–0.06 m`, this is `0.015–0.030 m²/s`, compared with the chosen physical-plus-model viscosity `0.003015 m²/s`. | Refinement changes the effective mixing appreciably. Fine-looking tracers cannot demonstrate pressure convergence. The estimate is a local truncation-error explanation, not a measured diffusivity for the full three-dimensional flow. |
| Wall drag and pressure projection impose different constraints | The provisional velocity is divided by `1 + dt mask / wall_tau`, then projected using a global periodic Poisson equation. The pressure correction can reintroduce velocity into wall cells. | Tiny discrete divergence verifies incompressibility in the periodic computational box; it does not verify impermeability of the masked room. |
| Wall and sheet leakage are finite | Baseline spray metadata report maximum mask-weighted speed `0.03346 m/s` and maximum normal sheet slip `0.02007 m/s`. The mask-weighted speed mixes transition and solid regions and is not a direct flux measurement through a physical wall. | A revised QA suite should measure integrated flux through specified closed surfaces and sheet-normal velocity, with tolerances tied to the relevant air-exchange rate. |
| The curtain is a broad porous friction layer | Gaussian normal width is `0.08 m`; traction is proportional to slip with coefficient `12 kg/(m² s)`. Significant support extends over several widths. Tangential and normal components are damped alike. | This is not an impermeable thin sheet. Its finite permeability and substantial regularization thickness affect the return-flow geometry. |
| Sampled pressure and applied load are different quantities | Pressure samples are taken at `curtain_x ± 0.16 m`; the coupled solver uses drag traction. Pressure averages use equal nodal weights, whereas traction averages use area quadrature. | The difference measured across `0.32 m` includes the ambient pressure gradient as well as the regularized curtain contribution. It should not be treated automatically as the pressure jump across a physical sheet. |
| Spray momentum is disconnected from droplets | The room solver uses a prescribed `0.8 m/s²` downward acceleration envelope. The separate droplet demonstration never feeds its drag to the air. | The room flow has no independently specified water mass-flow or droplet-size interpretation. This is the principal missing connection to the article. |
| Heat-source interpretation is limited | Heating relaxes air toward a 5 K rise inside the same source envelope, plus selected cooling and mixing terms. No water energy equation is solved. | The heating parameter is an air-source condition, not a water temperature. A revised model can retain an explicitly prescribed thermal boundary/source approximation, but must not silently reinterpret it as computed spray heat transfer. |
| The dynamic validity gate checks displacement only | The old run aborts at `max(abs(y)) > 0.45 m`; it does not check the slope required by the small-slope strip equation. | A localized modest displacement can already violate the approximation. Report maximum slope, not just displacement. |

For the fixed-curtain spray case at six seconds, the actual stored results are mean inward traction `−0.0100093 Pa` and sampled mean pressure difference `−0.0087701 Pa`. Heating alone gives `+0.0081685 Pa` and `+0.0074020 Pa`. The force direction is therefore not an animation artefact. A downward forced region can produce return flow and pressure changes near a curtain that depend on its location, openings and floor turning. The present audit does not identify one of those effects as the proven cause of the outward load.

The three-second mean spray/heating load changes from `−0.0126453 Pa` on 64 × 48 × 64 cells to `−0.0105692 Pa` on 80 × 60 × 80 cells. This is the previously reported approximately 19.6% change when normalized by the finer-grid magnitude. The two values do not establish asymptotic convergence, nor a discretization error estimate for the finest value.

## Recommended tractable replacement

Start with a fixed, genuinely impermeable curtain and fixed room geometry. Align the flat curtain with MAC faces and block normal velocity across those faces. Use the same blocked-face operator in the divergence, pressure Poisson matrix and pressure correction. That removes the porous-sheet coefficient and allows pressure on the two immediately adjacent fluid cells to supply the load to the existing strip model. Use matched faces/cells when integrating force. Room walls should use this same normal-flow constraint. Specify separately whether tangential velocity obeys slip or no slip, and use that choice consistently in the viscous operator.

A non-periodic, singular Neumann pressure system requires one gauge constraint per connected fluid region and a checked mass-compatibility condition. If the curtain has genuine openings, both sides ordinarily belong to one connected region. A reusable sparse/preconditioned solve is practical for moderate fixed grids; convergence must be judged from physical pressure-load changes as well as the algebraic residual. A smaller grid with correct boundaries and a declared reduced mixing model is preferable to investing all computation in the previous diffuse wall treatment.

Replace the imposed spray force by weighted droplet parcels. Each parcel represents a known liquid mass flux. Interpolate the air velocity to the parcel, integrate its drag/gravity equation, and deposit the equal-and-opposite drag momentum to the air with the same interpolation/spreading pair. Keep gravity on the liquid distinct from the force exerted on the gas. Remove droplets at explicit wall/body/floor interception surfaces, recording their remaining mass and momentum as transfers to those solids. If the droplet reaction step uses an exact or implicit drag update, deposit the momentum actually exchanged during that step, not an unrelated instantaneous force approximation.

Use deterministic or reproducibly seeded injection and resolve sensitivity to parcel count, injection cadence, time step and force-spreading width. A real droplet source requires much greater care than decorative particle tracks. The plots should distinguish liquid parcels from passive air tracers.

Use a higher-order bounded convection method if practical, or demonstrate pressure-load convergence with first-order upwinding. Adding a central stencil alone does not cure boundary, pressure sampling or forcing-model errors. A declared effective viscosity can be acceptable as a reduced closure, but its chosen value and variation must remain distinct from molecular air viscosity and physical input uncertainty.

After the fixed-curtain pressure has passed verification and sensitivity checks, apply its spatial and temporal load to the retained hanging-strip solver. This gives the required equation chain without immediately adding a difficult deforming-boundary solver. Report it as one-way coupling. Quantify whether displacement is small relative to the relevant openings and clearance; substantial movement requires a new geometry or two-way calculation before interpreting it as a self-consistent final curtain shape.

## Phase-1 QA acceptance questions

1. Does an isolated droplet reproduce a known drag solution, and does a closed exchange test conserve gas-plus-droplet momentum after accounting for gravity and prescribed exits?
2. Does a uniformly stratified, resting Boussinesq column produce the expected discrete pressure gradient without spurious circulation? Can a simple known pressure difference across a fixed partition be recovered with the correct force sign?
3. Are wall/curtain normal fluxes zero to the solver tolerance, and are opening fluxes globally compatible with incompressibility?
4. Does the pressure-load integral agree with an independent control-volume momentum balance, including transient air momentum, fluxes, body forces and wall forces where applicable?
5. Do representative pressure loads and strip displacements settle under refinement of grid, time step and droplet sampling separately? Evaluate integrated and localized loads; a stable net force can hide changing positive and negative patches.
6. Are onset transients separated from any claimed steady or time-averaged result? Use a stated averaging interval and report fluctuations; a snapshot at an arbitrary end time is not evidence of stationarity.
7. Do maximum curtain slopes and predicted geometry changes remain within the assumptions of the selected structural and coupling approximation?
8. Do visualized fields and parcel trajectories come directly from saved solver output, with no prescribed pressure depression or artistic inward displacement?

These checks do not require experimental fitting. They test whether the numerical results faithfully represent the declared equations for plausible physical inputs. Parameter sweeps can then establish whether inward motion occurs over a neighbourhood of those inputs, without treating one desired picture as a validation target.
