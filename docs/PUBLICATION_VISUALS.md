# Publication visuals: equations, sources, and reproduction

The five figures and five animations in `assets/` are generated by
`curtainflow/publication_visuals.py`. They distinguish analytic demonstrations,
computed airflow, and computed curtain mechanics. No image-generation model,
hand-drawn pressure field, or prescribed curtain trajectory supplies a result.

Run from the repository root:

```bash
python -m curtainflow.publication_visuals --only all
python -m curtainflow.publication_visuals --only verify
```

Python dependencies are pinned in `requirements.txt`; FFmpeg and ffprobe are
also required. The portable style helper is bundled at
`assets/style/messi_style.py`. It resolves DejaVu fonts through Matplotlib.
Static figures are 1600 × 900 pixels, with SVG counterparts. Animations are
1400 × 900 pixels, in MP4 and GIF, with a final-state PNG/SVG poster and a
three-state inspection sheet. The renderer writes into a private temporary
location, decodes the complete output, and only then replaces its public name.

## What each visual represents

| Asset | Equation or saved source | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `figure_01_geometry` | `data/grid_fine_both_config.json` | Specified 1.80 × 2.40 × 2.40 m enclosure and pressure sign. The vertical arrow is a schematic projection of the water direction. No bather is included. |
| `figure_02_prescribed_strip` | `mechanics.exact_profile` | Exact constant-pressure solution of `(T Y')' + Δp = 0`, with `T = g[σ(H−s)+m_h]`. Chosen Δp = 0.20 Pa gives 18.3486 cm without hem mass and 10.5706 cm for 0.10 kg/m hem mass. This is a benchmark, not an inferred shower pressure. |
| `figure_03_pressure_to_strip` | Fine-grid `pressure_load` arrays, averaged by `mean_over_window`; `mechanics.equilibrium` | The full signed load is averaged over 10–12 s and across width. Its height dependence drives the original strip equation. Because that equation is linear with common material inputs across width, this gives the width-mean equilibrium response. No load rescaling occurs. |
| `figure_04_mass_response` | `data/material_summary.json` and three `grid_fine_*.npz` sources | Saved fine-grid equilibrium results for four selected sheet/hem masses. The same calculated pressure is used in every material case. Values are width means, not local maxima. |
| `figure_05_parameter_study` | `data/results_summary.json` and each named coarse-grid flow archive | One input varied at a time. Mean pressure and static hem displacement use the 10–12 s averaging window. These are screening cases, without individual mesh-refinement studies. |
| `animation_01_droplet` | `dv/dt = g − k v²`, `dx/dt = v`, `k = 3ρ_a C_D/(4ρ_w d)` | A droplet released from rest in still air; DOP853 trajectory checked against the analytic tanh/log-cosh solution. Parameters: d = 1 mm, C_D = 0.47, ρ_a = 1.2 kg/m³, ρ_w = 1000 kg/m³, g = 9.81 m/s². The visible dot is enlarged. The enclosure solver instead uses variable Schiller–Naumann drag and evolving air. |
| `animation_02_vortex` | `v_θ = Ωr`, `p(r)−p(0)=ρ_a Ω²r²/2`, marker angle `θ(t)=θ(0)+Ωt` | Analytic solid-body rotating core, Ω = 1.2 rad/s, radius 0.50 m. Markers follow this exact velocity; curves remain steady. View along the axis, normal to the curtain. It is not a computed shower vortex. |
| `animation_03_thermal` | `Δp(z)=ρ_0 g(ΔT/T_0)(z_n−z)` | Parameter sweep ΔT = 0…5 K with T_0 = 295 K, ρ_0 = 1.2 kg/m³ and specified neutral level z_n = 1.00 m. It is a sequence of hydrostatic states, not a heat-transfer transient. |
| `animation_04_airflow` | Three `grid_fine_*.npz` arrays `pressure_yz`, `velocity_yz` | All 121 saved frames, t = 0…12 s. Actual slice x = 0.46875 m. Streamlines integrate the instantaneous projected y,z velocity, omitting its x component. They are not particle trajectories or 3D streamlines. |
| `animation_05_mean_load_release` | Three `release_grid_fine_*.npz` archives | Solutions of `σY_tt + cY_t = (T Y_s)_s + Δp`, including hem inertia and constant calculated 10–12 s mean pressure. Separate release clock τ = 0…6 s. Profiles come directly from saved structural states, every fifth saved sample. No airflow boundary motion or pressure rescaling. |

The analytical demonstrations are not substitutes for the final enclosure
calculation. Their role is to explain the terms of that calculation and the
mechanical response used afterward. The final airflow is a three-dimensional
incompressible calculation with explicit droplet drag and sensible-heat
exchange, an impermeable fixed curtain, and prescribed effective mixing.
See `MODEL.md`, `QA_REPORT.md`, and `RESULTS.md` for model assumptions,
numerical checks, and the limits of the reported quantities.

## Scales and timing

Airflow controls use one symmetric-log pressure scale over every frame and
control, with a linear interval of ±0.02 Pa. The maximum absolute saved pressure
sets its outer bounds; values are not clipped or smoothed. Pressure is relative
to the room mean. Nearest-neighbour pixels preserve the saved field. Streamline
direction arrows do not encode speed; each panel reports its largest in-plane
speed separately.

The droplet movie shows 0.5 seconds of physics over approximately 5 seconds of
playback. Vortex, airflow, and release animations use physical-time playback.
The thermal movie has no physical time axis. Each movie's frame coordinates and
frame rate are recorded in its manifest entry. The final state is supplied as a
separate poster for static publication.

Release profiles use equal horizontal and vertical metre scales. Each control
uses its fixed probe with the greatest excursion among nine sampled widths;
the selected width remains constant throughout the movie and is printed in the
legend. Faint time curves show the complete saved history, while opaque curves
show elapsed time. Dashed curves/levels show the static reference at the same
width and under the same load. The chosen pressure field stays constant; this
is a defined mechanics experiment, not the unresolved raw airflow-startup
response.

## Verification and provenance

`assets/analytic_checks.json` records these independent comparisons:

- DOP853 falling-droplet trajectory against its analytic solution, error below
  1e-9 in the displayed SI position/velocity coordinates.
- 4096-element equilibrium against the exact constant-pressure profile, maximum
  displacement error below 1e-6 m, with and without hem mass.
- Analytic vortex radial balance, finite-difference residual below 1e-10 Pa/m.
- Hydrostatic pressure gradient, residual below 1e-10 Pa/m, and zero pressure
  difference at the specified neutral level.

`assets/publication_manifest.json` lists source and output SHA-256 hashes for
every visual. Paths are relative to the repository root. The renderer checks
all those hashes, fully decodes PNG and GIF files, parses SVG, and asks FFmpeg to
decode every MP4 with errors treated as failures. GIF and MP4 frame counts must
match the declared coordinates. `assets/generation_source.json` records the
renderer, style, shared export helper, mechanics and averaging source hashes,
the verified media count, and the exact manifest hash.

These hashes attest to the delivered renderer and source arrays. They do not
reconstruct historical code versions from which an older array was first run.
The repository's broader numerical audit documents that distinction.

Visual inspection covers all five static figures and the beginning, middle,
and final state of each animation. The corrected Figure 3 annotation is outside
the plotting area; thermal column labels are clear of their heading. Numerical
labels, units, shared scales, signs, and selected widths agree with the saved
sources. The exact files delivered are then checked by the package preflight.

The Messi-style validator checks all 25 principal PNG, SVG, and GIF
assets; its results are recorded in `publication_visual_style_checks.json`.
SVG accessibility titles and descriptions are supplied explicitly, with
pixel dimensions matching their raster counterparts.

All 25 style checks pass. The validator gives one advisory for the droplet
GIF: its requested 12 fps differs from the style guide’s typical 8 fps. GIF
timing quantizes those frames to 80 ms; the on-frame physical clock remains
the authoritative time. The MP4 retains the requested 12 fps.
